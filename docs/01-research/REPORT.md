# Build Switchyard narrow, not as specced

**Verdict: build it, but not the product as currently described.** The demand is real. The top Claude Desktop multi-account request has about 1,186 reactions and 217 comments, and in nine months no Anthropic staff member has replied. About 20 indie tools have appeared to fill the gap. The weakness is that account isolation itself has become a commodity. It sells for $10–30 one-time, mostly on a single OS, with very little traction. It is also the feature Anthropic is most likely to absorb, since mobile multi-account switching reportedly started rolling out in September 2026. So what Switchyard can defend is the layer around isolation, not isolation itself: a single cross-platform control plane that knows which account is which, with Linux treated as a first-class platform, per-account settings editing, cross-account cost analytics down to the tool call, client budgets, and a safety preview before installing skills, plugins or MCP servers. Nothing in the market combines these today. The most underserved users are people with an employer Team/Enterprise seat plus a personal plan on Windows and Linux. The users most likely to pay are consultants and freelancers who need to split Claude spend across clients. Several parts of the spec should be cut or deferred. The cross-account "bridge" has no demand evidence and the most policy exposure. Taskbar identity on Windows and macOS is unproven. Anything that rotates accounts to stretch usage limits invites Anthropic enforcement. Treat the first version as a 6–8 week, Linux-first, open-core experiment with explicit kill criteria. Before writing product code, run about ten hands-on tests, starting with whether native cross-session messaging reaches sessions under a different `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`. *The terms and trademark sections below are research, not legal advice.*

## Roughly 2,250 reactions show demand, and it is mostly work-plus-personal separation

The core pain is easy to see. Issue #18435, "Add the ability to manage multiple Claude accounts within the Claude Desktop app", has **1,186 reactions (951 thumbs-up) and 217 comments**. Its latest comment is dated 2026-10-24, and **no Anthropic member has commented** since it was opened in January ([GitHub #18435](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/18435)). The mobile request, #36151, has another **1,067 reactions and 188 comments** ([GitHub #36151](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/36151)). The related request to connect multiple accounts of the same connector has **617 reactions** ([GitHub #27302](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/27302)). For scale, the repo's biggest issue (AGENTS.md support) has 6,688 reactions ([#6235](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/6235)). Multi-account is therefore a significant unmet cluster, though not a top-three one. Reaction counts are a floor, because most affected users never open GitHub.

Why people want it matters as much as how many want it. A keyword pass over all 217 comments on #18435 found **110 mentions of work/team/enterprise, 81 of "personal", 70 of privacy/separation/compliance, 23 of client/freelance/consulting, and only 18 of limits or quota** ([GitHub #18435](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/18435)). That is roughly 5–6x more separation talk than quota talk. The most-reacted comments come from IT administrators with a corporate seat plus a personal Max plan, from consultants, and from one user running "5 separate API workspaces (work org + personal + consulting)… separate cost tracking" who called the gap "a significant adoption blocker." Another user on #36151 asked for a different background colour per account "to prevent accidentally prompting the business account for personal" ([GitHub #36151](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/36151)). That last request is a concrete design requirement: strong visual identity for each account guards against sending data to the wrong account.

Usage stretching is a second, quieter source of demand. The most-starred CLI tool in the space is **claude-swap, with 4,016 stars**, and it is built around "automatic rate-limit rotation" across accounts ([GitHub](https://github.com/realiti4/claude-swap)). Pooling proxies such as teamclaude (446 stars) and the 12.7k-star claude-relay-service serve the same motive ([teamclaude](https://github.com/KarpelesLab/teamclaude); [claude-relay-service](https://github.com/Wei-Shaw/claude-relay-service)). By user count this segment may be larger than the separation segment. It is also the one most likely to draw Anthropic enforcement (see the terms section), so Switchyard should leave it alone.

Adjacent pains are larger than the core one. Usage and limit visibility has the strongest signals in the ecosystem: #16157 on hitting Max limits instantly has **726 reactions and 1,499 comments** ([GitHub #16157](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/16157)), and usage tools have five-figure star counts, including ccusage at 19.2k and CodexBar at 22.8k ([ccusage](https://github.com/ccusage/ccusage); [CodexBar](https://github.com/steipete/CodexBar)). Syncing skills between Desktop and Code has 171 reactions ([#20697](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/20697)). Cross-machine config sync has almost no demand (2 reactions, auto-closed) ([#36693](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/36693)). **No direct demand signal was found for cross-account session messaging.**

The size of the multi-account population cannot be measured directly. Anthropic reportedly reached a **~$65B run-rate by end of July 2026** ([Bloomberg Gov](https://news.bgov.com/financial-accounting/anthropic-revenue-run-rate-surpasses-65-billion-ahead-of-ipo)). Claude Code passed **$2.5B in run-rate in February 2026, and business subscriptions quadrupled** in the first six weeks of the year ([Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/anthropics-claude-code-revenue-doubled-jan-1)). However, Anthropic publishes no count of Claude Code users, Max subscribers or seats. Here is an inference to treat as an assumption, not a finding. If Team/Enterprise seats keep growing and many seat holders also pay for a personal plan, people with both a work and a personal account plausibly number in the hundreds of thousands. Of those, the online footprint suggests tens of thousands are actively frustrated.

## Consultants pay, and Windows and Linux dual-account users are the most underserved

Three segments stand out, and they differ in what they need and what they will pay.

| Segment | Size signal | Served today? | Willingness to pay | Fit for Switchyard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Employer seat + personal plan, **Desktop** users, Windows/Linux | Largest share of #18435 comments | Barely: most Desktop tools are macOS-only; Linux is beta-quality at best | Low–medium ($10–30 one-time is the proven band) | Entry point and top of funnel |
| Consultants / freelancers / agencies with per-client accounts | ~10% of #18435 comments; new tools like Hatswap target them | Not served: no per-client cost attribution anywhere | **Highest**: they bill clients and already pay $100–200/mo for Max | **Paying core** |
| Limit-rotators stacking Max plans | claude-swap 4k stars | Well served by free CLI tools | Low (they prefer free tools) | Avoid: ToS and ban risk |
| CLI-only multi-account users | Many guides and switchers | Solved officially by `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` | ~Zero ("2 lines of shell") | Free feature only |

The consultant case rests on comments such as "IT for an organization and also run my own consulting business" and the explicit request for "separate cost tracking" ([GitHub #18435](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/18435)). A new tool, Hatswap, launched in October 2026 on exactly the pitch "one Claude Code account per client" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50075273)). These users can justify a subscription on ROI: correct per-client allocation and invoice export save billable time. No pricing survey supports this, so it is an inference that needs validation.

CLI-only multi-account is effectively worthless as a product. Boris Cherny, the Claude Code lead, closed the profile requests as "completed" on 2026-08-17 with the alias `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/.claude-work claude` and the comment that a `--profile` flag "would be a thin wrapper over the same mechanism" ([GitHub #27359](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/27359)). Commenters on the most-discussed Show HN in the space (54 points) said they handle it with two-line aliases ([HN 49111019](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49111019)).

On platforms, coverage is thinner than it first looks. Linux has some coverage, but it is weak. DeckSpace's Claude Accounts has a **Linux beta that can sign in only one account at a time** ([deckspace.dev](https://deckspace.dev/claude-accounts/)). Personae claims Linux support in an aggregator listing ([stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/en/personae)). The best-known OSS launcher, claude-multiprofile, handles Desktop only on macOS ([GitHub](https://github.com/jmdarre-v/claude-multiprofile)). Claude Desktop for Linux only entered beta around 2026-06-30 ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/anthropic-launches-claude-desktop-beta-for-linux-users)). Linux is therefore a real but small wedge. It is useful for building credibility and community. It is not large enough to carry a business alone.

## Isolation is a $10–30 commodity, but no one ties accounts to analytics and safety

The field divides into a crowded commodity layer and a white space nobody occupies.

**The commodity layer, Desktop multi-instance.** At least five paid apps sell essentially the same `--user-data-dir` trick. Claude Desktop Manager is Windows-only, with a free tier capped at 15 minutes of parallel use, Standard at $10 and Pro at $30, all one-time. Its Gumroad page shows only **5 ratings** ([claudeprofiles.com](https://claudeprofiles.com/)). Multi-Claude is macOS-only at $20 one-time ([multiclaude.app](https://multiclaude.app/)). DeckSpace Claude Accounts costs $14 and runs on macOS and Windows plus the Linux beta ([deckspace.dev](https://deckspace.dev/claude-accounts/)). Multi Instance for Claude Desktop is a $9.49 Microsoft Store app ([Microsoft Store](https://www.microsoft.com/en-bs/p/multi-instance-for-claude-desktop/9ng247tj47p0)). Beyond these there is a long tail of free OSS launchers with fewer than 30 stars. The most popular of them, claude-multiprofile, has 133 stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/jmdarre-v/claude-multiprofile)). The low traction, cheap one-time prices and free alternatives mean **isolation alone will not support a business**. Competitors are also climbing the stack. Claude Desktop Manager already offers per-profile MCP editing, a Claude Code sessions board and backups ([claudeprofiles.com](https://claudeprofiles.com/)).

**The giant next door.** cc-switch has **148,554 stars**. It is a **Tauri 2 + Rust + React** app for Windows, macOS and Linux that manages API providers, a unified MCP panel across 12 tools, skills installation, prompts, a usage dashboard and cloud sync. It **does not support multiple Claude subscription OAuth accounts and does not manage Claude Desktop** ([GitHub](https://github.com/farion1231/cc-switch)). That makes it both the strongest proof that a cross-platform Claude "control panel" draws huge interest and the most dangerous potential entrant. If it adds multi-account Claude support, it ships to an enormous installed base overnight. The stack matches the one you plan to use, so treat cc-switch as an architecture reference and possibly as a future integration partner.

**Analytics is commoditized for a single account and empty across accounts.** ccusage covers daily, weekly, monthly, session, 5-hour-block, per-project and per-model reports across about 18 agent CLIs, with no documented multi-account support ([GitHub](https://github.com/ccusage/ccusage)). claude-swap shows quota but not cost attribution ([GitHub](https://github.com/realiti4/claude-swap)). Anthropic's official analytics serve Team/Enterprise admins with per-user spend and lines of code, not an individual juggling a personal account and client accounts ([code.claude.com analytics](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/analytics)). No tool was found that rolls up account → project → session → tool call, attributes tokens to the MCP server, skill or hook that burned them, or tracks client budgets.

**The ecosystem is huge and has no safety layer.** Skill packs install hooks and commands at enormous scale: obra/superpowers has ~305k stars, everything-claude-code ~289k and gstack ~138k ([superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers); [ECC](https://github.com/affaan-m/everything-claude-code); [gstack](https://github.com/garrytan/gstack)). Managers such as cc-switch and skills-manager (6.8k stars) install and sync this content, but **none found shows a diff of permissions, hooks, env or network access before install** ([skills-manager](https://github.com/xingkongliang/skills-manager)). Among the planned features, this is the differentiator least exposed to Anthropic's roadmap and most clearly valuable.

**Session bridging became a platform feature in August 2026.** Claude Code v2.1.224+ ships native `ListAgents`/`SendMessage`, `/peers`, `@session` mentions and `notify_when_idle`. Same-machine messages travel over a per-session Unix socket or named pipe restricted to the OS user. Messages are plain text only, capped at about 1M characters, and incoming messages can never approve prompts or change config ([code.claude.com cross-session messaging](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cross-session-messaging)). Third-party bridges are tiny, and one has publicly acknowledged that native messaging now exists ([claude-intercom](https://socket.dev/npm/package/claude-intercom/overview/2.0.0)). Two things remain undocumented: whether discovery crosses `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` boundaries, and whether richer handoffs (summary plus diff plus files) have a place. Native Remote Control is tied to a single claude.ai sign-in, so cross-account cloud handoff is definitely not native.

## Claude Code isolation is official, while Desktop identity is the fragile layer

The technical picture divides along the Code/Desktop line. **Claude Code multi-account is officially supported.** The authentication docs tell users to "give each account its own configuration directory… set the `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` environment variable." Credentials sit in `.credentials.json` under that directory on Linux and Windows. On macOS the Keychain entry is "keyed to that directory too" ([code.claude.com authentication](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication)). Two traps remain. Console keyless sign-ins live outside the config dir. A stray `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN` or `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` in the environment takes precedence over `/login` OAuth, which collapses every profile onto one credential ([authentication](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication)). Switchyard must scrub these variables from every process it spawns.

**Desktop multi-instance via `--user-data-dir` works on all three OSes and is entirely unofficial.** You have already proved it on Ubuntu. On macOS it works via `open -n -a Claude --args --user-data-dir=…` ([claude-multiprofile](https://github.com/jmdarre-v/claude-multiprofile)). Windows is harder because of MSIX: launching the .exe from `WindowsApps` fails with an access error. Existing tools either `robocopy` the app to a portable folder, which then never auto-updates, or use the Store's `claude-desktop.exe` execution alias, a single-source claim that needs verification ([claude-desktop-multi](https://github.com/sypnose-cloud/claude-desktop-multi/tree/main); [DEV article](https://dev.to/sebastian_balog_c88dc2913/how-to-run-multiple-claude-accounts-at-once-desktop-claude-code-and-why-the-obvious-hacks-break-19jb)). Every real breakage documented so far came from workarounds layered on top of the flag: the self-updater resetting tinted macOS copies to the default account, launcher timing on macOS 27, a stale LaunchServices cache, and deep-link misrouting ([claude-multiprofile](https://github.com/jmdarre-v/claude-multiprofile)).

| Capability | Linux (GNOME Wayland) | Windows | macOS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isolated Desktop instance | **Proven by you** | Works via alias or portable copy; MSIX ACL; medium risk | Proven (`open -n`); low–medium risk |
| Separate dock/taskbar identity | X11 + WM_CLASS proven; **impossible on native Wayland** (app_id hardcoded) | **Unproven**: would need cross-process AUMID override; high risk | Launcher yes; running window groups under Claude unless re-signed clone (breaks signature) |
| Sign-in deep links (`claude://`) | Last registrant wins; serialize logins | Same | Bundle-ID routing; serialize logins |
| Claude Code isolation | Official | Official | Official |
| Per-account analytics | OTel official, JSONL unofficial | Same | Same |
| Global hotkeys | Needs XDG GlobalShortcuts portal | Fine | Fine |

Three items in this matrix deserve emphasis.

First, **deep-link sign-in is the biggest functional fragility.** `claude://` goes to whichever instance registered last on Windows and Linux, or by bundle ID on macOS, so Google sign-in can land in the wrong window. Every tool works around this with "log in with the others closed" or email-code login ([claude-multiprofile](https://github.com/jmdarre-v/claude-multiprofile); [claude-desktop-multi](https://github.com/sypnose-cloud/claude-desktop-multi/tree/main)). Tauri 2 could register itself as the `claude://` handler and forward links to the right instance at runtime on Windows and Linux, but "registering deep links at runtime is not possible on macOS" ([Tauri deep-linking docs](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri-docs/blob/v2/src/content/docs/plugin/deep-linking.mdx)). Hijacking Anthropic's scheme is fragile and hostile to Anthropic's app. A guided, serialized sign-in flow followed by an account-identity check is the right design.

Second, **separate taskbar identity per account should not be promised outside Linux X11.** On macOS, a running window's separate Dock identity requires a re-signed clone. Such a clone fails `codesign --verify --deep --strict` and breaks Keychain access ([claude-multiprofile](https://github.com/jmdarre-v/claude-multiprofile)). On Windows, a window-level AppUserModelID overrides the process-level one ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/windows/win32/shell/samples-appusermodelidwindowproperty)). Nobody has shown that setting it on Claude's Electron windows from outside actually works. Use coloured window badges, titles and a strong launcher identity instead. Even your own Linux trick costs native Wayland rendering, because it forces `--ozone-platform=x11`.

Third, **analytics are well supported through official telemetry.** Claude Code exports OTel metrics and events, including `claude_code.api_request` with `model`, an estimated `cost_usd` and token counts, plus attributes `session.id`, `user.account_uuid` and `user.email` ([code.claude.com monitoring](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/monitoring-usage)). Setting `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_TELEMETRY=1` and a local OTLP endpoint in each profile's settings `env`, pointed at a collector embedded in Switchyard, gives account attribution for free. JSONL transcripts under `<config>/projects/…` record "every message, tool call, and tool result", but they have no documented schema and are deleted after 30 days by default ([.claude directory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-directory)). Use them as a fallback, read through a parser that tolerates format changes. One gap matters: **Claude Desktop chat usage (as opposed to Code) has no telemetry path**, so "deep analytics" in practice means Claude Code analytics.

Tauri 2 itself is a sound choice. It has official tray, autostart, single-instance, deep-link and signed-updater plugins, and cc-switch proves the stack at scale ([Tauri docs](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri-docs/blob/v2/src/content/docs/plugin/updater.mdx); [cc-switch](https://github.com/farion1231/cc-switch)). The weak spot is global shortcuts on GNOME Wayland. The plugin is X11-centric, and the portal-based route needs work ([tauri discussion #10017](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/discussions/10017)). A CLI such as `switchyard open work` that users bind to a GNOME custom shortcut is a robust fallback.

## Terms permit separation but not rotation, token handling or UI automation

*This section is not legal advice; verify the quoted text at the source before relying on it.*

**No Anthropic document found forbids one person from holding several accounts for legitimate reasons.** The Consumer Terms forbid sharing credentials and making "your Account available to anyone else". They also bar automated or "non-human means" except via API key or "where we otherwise explicitly permit it", and they bar "bypassing any of our systems or protective measures" ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). The Usage Policy forbids creating multiple accounts "to avoid detection" or to circumvent a ban, and forbids intentionally bypassing "capabilities, restrictions, or guardrails" ([AUP](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). One caution: the AUP page shows an effective date of Nov 12, 2026, so recheck it after that date.

The Claude Code legal page is the most relevant document. It says Anthropic does not permit third parties "to offer Claude.ai login into their own applications, or to route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials", and that developers "may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens". It also explicitly preserves "an end user… signing in to the unmodified Claude Code binary with their own Claude subscription" ([code.claude.com legal](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). The same page notes that advertised Pro and Max limits "assume ordinary, individual usage."

Anthropic enforces these rules. On 2026-01-09 it blocked third-party harnesses that spoofed Claude Code, and some accounts were mistakenly banned in the process ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-cracks-down-on-unauthorized-claude-usage-by-third-party-harnesses)). In February 2026 it clarified that subscription OAuth is only for Claude Code and Claude.ai ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/02/20/anthropic-clarifies-ban-on-third-party-tool-access-to-claude/5014546)). Secondary sources report that legal demands to OpenCode followed in March ([falcao.org](https://falcao.org/posts/anthropic-claude-access-crackdown-ecosystem-fallout/)).

The enforcement so far has targeted three behaviours: spoofing the client, reusing tokens, and reselling capacity. A Switchyard that launches unmodified official binaries and never touches tokens falls outside all three. Three of the planned features come close to the line, though:

| Feature | Risk | Rule for Switchyard |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-rotating accounts when limits hit | Arguably "bypass… restrictions"; outside "ordinary, individual usage" | **Do not build; do not market "more quota"** |
| Bridge that injects prompts into Desktop sessions | "Automated or non-human means" (§3.7); Desktop has no automation carve-out | **Never automate the Desktop UI.** Relay only via documented Claude Code interfaces (hooks, MCP, messaging socket) with a human trigger |
| Reading/cloning credentials, cookies, Keychain entries when creating profiles | "Collect, store, or intermediate… credentials or session tokens" | Never read, copy, back up or sync them; each profile signs in fresh through Anthropic's flow |
| Accounts belonging to different people | "Make your Account available to anyone else" | Require that all managed accounts belong to the user |
| Team/Enterprise seats | Admin policy and Commercial Terms may restrict further (not fetched) | Never edit managed/enterprise policy files; respect them |

Users still carry account risk, because Anthropic "may [enforce] without prior notice" ([code.claude.com legal](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). HN threads show real fear of bans and a "draconian" appeals process, even among people who only separate work and personal accounts ([HN 49111019](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49111019)). Turn this into an advantage. A plainly stated "compliance by construction" design should be part of the positioning: Switchyard has no network calls to Anthropic of its own, never touches tokens, launches unmodified binaries, does no rotation and does no UI automation.

**Trademark.** Do not put "Claude" in the product name, logo, domain, bundle ID or package name. The Claude Code page allows plain-text factual statements but forbids using the names "as part of your own product, feature, or company name, in your own logo" ([code.claude.com legal](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). Anthropic asked Clawdbot to rename in January 2026 ([Laravel News](https://laravel-news.com/index.php/clawdbot-rebrands-to-moltbot-after-trademark-request-from-anthropic)). "Switchyard" is fine. Use copy like "an unofficial manager for Claude Desktop and Claude Code accounts" plus the disclaimer "not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic; Claude is a trademark of Anthropic, PBC". Avoid "works with Claude" badges and anything resembling the starburst.

**Roadmap risk is high and already partly realized.** Users on #36151 reported an "add account" button on Android and iOS around 2026-09-13 (app 1.260911.19), with one report of a 3-account cap, and noted that macOS Desktop was not yet covered ([GitHub #36151](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/36151)). The demand notes still list that issue as open, so the rollout looks gradual and unannounced. Separately, Anthropic has said a Code `--profile` flag would be a thin wrapper, so it is cheap to ship ([#27359](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/27359)). Precedent points the same way. Anthropic redesigned Claude Code on desktop with a multi-session sidebar ([Ben's Bites](https://news.bensbites.co/posts/63975-anthropic-redesigns-claude-code-on-desktop-adding-a-sidebar-for-managing-multiple-sessions-a-drag-and-drop-layout-an-integrated-terminal-and-a-file-editor)), shipped native cross-session messaging, and closed off the OpenCode subscription path. Companion apps such as Crystal (deprecated February 2026) and opcode (stalled) have short lives ([Crystal](https://github.com/stravu/crystal); [opcode](https://github.com/winfunc/opcode)). Plan for native Desktop multi-account within months. Features likely to survive it include cross-account cost analytics with client budgets, settings and permission control across accounts, ecosystem safety, and Linux polish. Anthropic has little incentive to build any of them for individuals.

## Open-core with a $8–12/month consultant tier is the only model that pencils out

The comparables show two models. Individual multi-account apps sell cheap one-time licenses ($9.49–30). Claude GUIs give the individual app away and charge for team or cloud layers, as with Nimbalyst Teams at $20/user/month and Conductor's reported $50/month Pro ([nimbalyst.com](https://nimbalyst.com/blog/best-claude-code-gui-tools-2026/); [superset.sh](https://superset.sh/compare/conductor-vs-crystal)). Donations do not work. ccusage, with roughly 17k stars, showed a single sponsor in its last available snapshot ([sponsors.ecosyste.ms](https://sponsors.ecosyste.ms/accounts/ryoppippi)). Developer tools convert free users to paid at around **1–3%**, with 3–5% considered good ([Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/whats-the-right-ratio-of-free-to-paid-users-in-developer-saas); [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion)).

The arithmetic decides the pricing model. Ten thousand active free users at 3% gives 300 buyers. At $29 one-time that is about $8.7K once. At $8/month it is about $2.4K MRR. One-time pricing cannot sustain a product that must chase every Claude Desktop release on three OSes. The recommended structure follows.

| Tier | Price | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Free (OSS, MIT/Apache)** | $0 | Isolated Desktop + Code profiles on Linux/Windows/macOS, account colours/badges, guided serialized sign-in, env hygiene, basic per-account settings editor, single-account usage view |
| **Pro** | ~$8–12/mo or ~$79–99/yr | Cross-account analytics (account → project → session → tool call), client/project budgets and alerts, CSV/invoice export, settings templates and diff across accounts, ecosystem safety preview with risk scoring |
| **Team (later)** | ~$15–20/seat/mo | Shared policy and config packs, curated allow-listed skill/MCP catalogs, sync (requires a server) |

Keep paid prices above about $15 per transaction where possible, for example by pushing annual plans, because the 50-cent fixed fee charged by merchants of record (5% + 50c at Lemon Squeezy and Paddle) eats over 5% of a $9 sale ([refgrow](https://refgrow.com/tools/saas-payment-fees); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/cheapest-merchant-of-record)).

Fixed costs are low: Apple Developer at **$99/year** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/compare-memberships)) plus a domain. If you are based in MENA, there is a real Windows constraint. Microsoft Artifact Signing ($9.99/month) is open to **individual developers only in the USA and Canada** ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/trusted-signing/quickstart); [Azure pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/pricing/details/artifact-signing/)). Realistic Windows routes are the Microsoft Store (which signs and handles payment), a commercial OV certificate, or a company in an eligible jurisdiction. Lemon Squeezy pays out to banks in Egypt, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Morocco and most of the Gulf, but not Iraq, Lebanon, Libya, Syria, Yemen or Sudan ([Lemon Squeezy](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries)). Check your own country before choosing a merchant of record. Linux needs no signing, which is another reason to start there.

Distribution follows what has worked for neighbours. ccusage grew through a Simon Willison link and GitHub trending ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/14/ccusage/)). Claude Desktop Manager uses SEO how-to posts and free licenses in exchange for social posts ([claudeprofiles.com blog](https://claudeprofiles.com/blog/)). Rivals advertise directly in #18435 and #70665 ([GitHub #70665](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/70665)). If you post there, do it once, transparently, and add something useful. Add Show HN, r/ClaudeAI, the awesome-claude-code lists (56k stars) and Linux communities ([awesome-claude-code](https://github.com/hesreallyhim/awesome-claude-code)).

## Recommendation: an account-aware control plane, Linux-first, with kill criteria

**Positioning.** Lead with: "Keep work, personal and client Claude accounts separate, see what each one costs, and know what a plugin will do before you install it." Do not lead with "more quota", and do not lead with "run multiple Claudes", because that is the $10 commodity. Your primary buyer is the consultant or freelancer. Your primary acquisition audience is the dual-account Desktop user on Linux and Windows. Make compliance by construction a visible feature.

**MVP scope (6–8 weeks, Linux first, then Windows, macOS last).** The first item is profiles: an isolated Desktop instance plus a matching `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, both launched from one card. Each card gets a colour, an icon and a window badge. Sign-in is a guided flow that quits the other instances, prefers email-code login and checks the account afterwards, and spawned processes get env scrubbing. Second, a per-account settings editor for `settings.json`: model, effort, `permissions` allow/ask/deny, `defaultMode`, `hooks`, `env`, `enabledPlugins` and `claude_desktop_config.json` MCP entries. It should validate against the JSON schema, show a diff before saving, write atomically with backups, and edit Desktop config only while that instance is stopped. Third, per-account OTel ingestion into a local SQLite store, with views for account, project, session and model, plus a client/project budget tag. JSONL-based tool-call attribution should follow as a Pro feature once the parser test passes. Fourth, a read-only safety preview that parses a skill, plugin or MCP package and lists the hooks, commands, permissions, env vars and network endpoints it would add, before anything is installed. Leave Windows AUMID hacks, macOS re-signed clones, `claude://` hijacking, cloud sync and the bridge out of v1.

**On the bridge specifically.** Do not build your own transport. If test 1 below shows that native `SendMessage` already crosses config dirs, Switchyard's job shrinks to a UI over `/peers`, which is cheap to add and needs no special access. If messaging does not cross config dirs, build at most a human-triggered "handoff pack": summary, git diff and file list, written to disk and opened in the target account's Claude Code session via a documented interface. Ship it only after the core has users asking for it, because no demand evidence for it was found.

**What to avoid.** No auto-rotation or limit pooling. No Desktop UI automation. No reading, copying or syncing of tokens, cookies or Keychain entries. No modified or repackaged Anthropic binaries by default. No "Claude" in the name or logo. No network calls to Anthropic endpoints. No prompt content in telemetry. And no attempt to out-feature cc-switch on provider management or ccusage on single-account reports.

**Kill or pivot criteria.** Set these up front. If 60 days after an OSS launch you have fewer than roughly 300–500 GitHub stars or a waitlist of fewer than 100 people interested in Pro, or if Anthropic ships Desktop multi-account and Pro conversion stays under 1%, stop investing in isolation. At that point, refocus the analytics, budgets and safety layer as a standalone tool, which works fine with one account per OS user, or offer it to cc-switch's ecosystem.

**Hands-on tests before writing product code:**

1. **Cross-account native messaging.** On Claude Code v2.1.224+, start `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/.claude-a claude` and `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/.claude-b claude`, signed into two different accounts, in two terminals. Run `/peers` in each and attempt `SendMessage` in both directions. Inspect the socket directory (`$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` or `/tmp/cc-socks-$UID`) and each config dir's `sessions/` to see where discovery is scoped. Repeat with `crossSessionInbound: "hold"`. Then post a JSON line from an external script using `CLAUDE_CODE_MESSAGING_SOCKET`/`TOKEN` captured from a hook.
2. **Desktop-launched Code sessions.** Launch a second Desktop instance with `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` set in its environment. Confirm that its Code tab writes transcripts to that directory and uses that account, not `~/.claude`.
3. **OTel per profile.** Put `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_TELEMETRY=1` and an OTLP endpoint in each profile's `settings.json` `env`, run a local collector, and confirm that `user.account_uuid`, `session.id` and `cost_usd` arrive separately for each account.
4. **JSONL tool-call attribution.** Check whether transcripts carry per-message `usage` token fields that can be joined to `tool_use` blocks. If they can, tool-call-level cost becomes feasible. If not, the feature is limited to the session level.
5. **Deep-link sign-in.** With two instances open, attempt Google sign-in in the second and record which window receives `claude://`. Then confirm that the email-code flow avoids the problem.
6. **Desktop config rewrites.** Edit `claude_desktop_config.json` while the instance runs, add a connector through the UI, and see whether your edit survives.
7. **Wayland without X11.** Measure what you lose (scaling, IME) under `--ozone-platform=x11`. Check whether a native-Wayland window can still be told apart by title or badge. Test the GlobalShortcuts portal on your GNOME version.
8. **Env collision.** Export `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` and confirm that it overrides a profile's OAuth login, which proves the scrubbing requirement.
9. **Windows and macOS spot-checks** (VM or borrowed machine). Test whether `claude-desktop.exe` accepts `--user-data-dir` from the Store alias, and whether `open -n` instances survive an auto-update. Also run two simultaneous instances with Cowork enabled.
10. **Demand check.** Interview 8–10 consultants from the #18435 thread or your network about per-client Claude cost tracking, and put up a landing page with a Pro waitlist before building budgets.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. It is no longer whether people need multiple Claude accounts, since they clearly do and Anthropic is slowly getting there itself. The real question is what remains valuable once account switching is native. The answer is the layer only a neutral, local tool can provide: one place to see and control what each account costs, what it is allowed to do, and what third-party code it runs. Multi-instance is the acquisition hook and a temporary moat, while analytics, budgets and safety previews are the product. Your proven Linux setup is a genuine head start on the least-served platform, but Linux alone cannot carry a business. Plan the Windows path early, and make it Store-distributed if you are outside the USA and Canada.

The most important unknown is cheap to resolve. Whether native messaging crosses `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` boundaries decides whether the bridge is a free UI feature or a separate project that no evidence yet justifies. Run that test this week, together with the OTel and JSONL checks that determine how deep "deep analytics" can really go, before committing to the full spec.
